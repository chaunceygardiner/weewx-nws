6.1.3 (pending)
- In the dark theme, the lines between things and the outlines of the
  controls are as visible as they are in light.  The rules between the 7 Day
  rows and the Hourly rows, the underlines of both column heads and of the
  page title, the rule beside Right now's figures, the rules within an alert
  card and over the page footer, and the outlines of the day tabs, the nav
  tabs, the response chip and the "begins later" badge were all fainter
  than in light, and six of them scored APCA Lc 0: nearly invisible.  Each
  now scores what it scores in light, measured against the card or page it
  sits on.  Light is unchanged, as are the card outlines and the chart
  gridlines.
- On a phone, the day and hour labels under the charts no longer touch
  the lines above them.  On both Hourly charts the tops of the letters sat
  a fifth of a pixel under the rain strip at 390px wide, and on the 7 Day
  chart about a pixel under the plot's floor; each label is now placed
  from that line, with about two pixels between them down to 320px.  The
  charts are slightly taller for it at every width.
- On a phone, the box that reads out the hour under a finger on a chart no
  longer runs into the 7 Day chart's day labels.  It is sized in page
  pixels while the chart shrinks with the screen, so at 390px its bottom
  edge met the labels and at 360px it covered their tops; it is now a
  little smaller there, and ends above them down to 320px.

6.1.2 09/14/2026
- The 7 Day page's temperature chart names its two halves with a pair of
  filled labels -- PAST 7 DAYS ACTUAL in the recorded curve's gray, FORECASTED
  TEMPERATURES in the forecast curve's red -- one each side of the dividing
  rule, matched in width.  Each box carries the color of the line it names, so
  the label is also the key; the two bare words it replaces, OBSERVED and
  FORECAST, read as one phrase at chart size and the full-height rule was
  already a patch for that.  They also count: the recorded half is as wide as
  the archive is long, so a station that has been up three days now says so
  rather than claiming a week it does not have, and the sentence under the
  chart and the description a screen reader is given count the same days.  A
  station with less than about five and a half days behind it gets no labels
  at all -- a matched pair centered on the rule cannot be dropped one at a
  time, and one alone would straddle the rule it is meant to stand beside --
  and there the caption is what names the halves.  A station whose sensor has
  died counts only the hours it actually recorded: the chart still draws the
  whole week, hole and all, but nothing claims to have measured it.
- The words naming the two halves can now be read by a screen reader at all.
  They were text inside the chart, and the chart is marked up as a single
  image with one description, which hides everything drawn inside it -- so the
  two labels saying which half was a measurement and which a prediction were
  reachable by no route.  They are ordinary text beside the chart now.
- The caption under the 7 Day page's temperature chart no longer promises dew
  point and rain.  It ended with a link reading "Hour by hour, with dew point
  and rain", meaning the Hourly page -- but sitting at the end of a paragraph
  about the chart above it, it read as a claim about that chart.  That chart
  draws temperature, and nothing else.  The link is gone entirely: the menubar
  has carried Hourly on every page all along, and this was the only inline
  cross-page link in the skin.  The caption is now one sentence naming what
  the two halves of the chart are.
- The sample report's colors now clear the stricter of two contrast
  measures, WCAG 2 and APCA, for every piece of text and every chart line
  and marker on every ground it lands on, in both themes.  Text must reach
  a WCAG ratio of 4.5 and an APCA Lc of 60, whatever its size; lines and
  markers a reader has to see, 3.0 and Lc 30.  The WCAG ratio alone had
  passed the whole dark palette while APCA scored most of its text too
  faint -- the dimmest gray at Lc 35.  In dark mode the text, alert
  severity and chart colors are all noticeably lighter as a result.  In
  light mode two colors moved: the page title's blue is a shade darker,
  and the chart axes and the 7 Day chart's dividing rule are a darker
  gray.
- Alerts now keep three more things NWS sends: the areas an alert covers
  (areaDesc), CAP's recommended action (response), and NWS's whole
  parameters object.  $nwsforecast.alerts() returns all three, the last as
  a dict.  The sample report's alert cards show the first two: a chip with
  the action in plain words (ACT NOW, TAKE SHELTER, AVOID) and a line naming
  the areas covered, with a long list folded behind its count.  About four
  alerts in ten have no WHERE section in their description, so this line
  is often the only place the areas appear.  The parameters are stored for
  later use; nothing shows them yet.  This is a schema change, and the
  database rebuilds itself on the first start, as it has since 6.1: the
  next poll refills it.
- Fixed: an alert carrying more than one NWS headline kept only the last,
  and one whose headline list was empty was dropped as malformed.  Every
  headline is now kept, in order.
- Fixed: an alert that gives no onset showed its start as N/A.  Its window
  bar was already drawn from the message's effective time, which is when
  CAP says such an alert takes effect, but the time printed at the start of
  the bar was the missing onset.  It is now the effective time, marked
  "(effective)" the way a missing end is marked "(expires)".
- Fixed: an alert whose end falls at or before its start -- nothing NWS
  means to send -- now shows both times instead of saying "no end time
  given".

6.1.1 09/10/2026
- The seven-day chart's dividing rule is drawn where the boundary actually
  is: BETWEEN the last reading and the first forecast hour, clear of both by
  the same amount.  Drawn on the first forecast point it touched one curve
  while standing a full hour from the other, so the gap read as a mistake on
  one side only.  The rule now also runs the full height of the chart, label
  band included -- stopped at the top of the plot it left OBSERVED and
  FORECAST side by side with nothing between them, reading as one phrase.
- Fixed: the chart's top temperature could lose its leading digit on a
  phone, showing ".00" for 100 degrees.  The gutter reserved for the axis
  labels was sized for a two-digit reading plus a minus sign; three digits
  are wider, and a hundred-degree afternoon is not unusual.  Measured in a
  browser this time rather than estimated -- and then given room to spare,
  because the measurement is of whatever font the machine doing the
  measuring substitutes, and a reader's machine may substitute a wider one.
- weewx-nws needs a SQLite database, and now says so instead of failing with
  a MySQL syntax error.  The schema declares STRING columns, which weedb
  passes to the database verbatim and MySQL has no such type -- so pointing
  [NWS] data_binding at a MySQL database made the very first CREATE TABLE a
  syntax error, and because that happens while the service is starting it
  took weewxd down with it: the whole station stopped, not just this
  extension.  The binding's driver is now checked first.  A station whose
  binding is SQLite, which is what the installer writes and what every
  working station has, sees no change at all.

6.1 09/10/2026
- ACTION REQUIRED for any skin of your own that prints these tags.  DELETE
  THE "$unit.label..." YOU APPEND AFTER .format().  Until 6.1 the tags carried
  no formatter, so .format('%.0f') returned a bare "71" and every example in
  the documentation told you to append the label yourself.  It now returns
  "71degF", and the old idiom prints "71degF degF".  For a range, suppress the
  FIRST label instead of the second:
      $hour.windSpeed.format('%.0f', add_label=False) to
          $hour.windSpeed2.format('%.0f')
  The sample skin never used that idiom, so an unmodified NWSReport needs
  nothing.  The documentation -- Tags, Fields and Recipes -- is updated.
- ACTION REQUIRED for a metric skin that worked around the units bug below.
  Asking for a conversion explicitly -- $hour.outTemp.degree_C,
  $hour.windSpeed.km_per_hour -- is still correct and needs no change, because
  converting Celsius to Celsius does nothing.  A skin that hard-coded a degF
  label, or did its own arithmetic on .raw to reach metric, must drop that
  workaround or it will now convert twice.
- ACTION REQUIRED only for a skin that reads $alert.ends directly.  For an
  alert NWS gave no end for, $alert.ends.raw is now None, where it used to be
  a copy of the alert's expiry; $alert.onset.raw can likewise be None now.
  The tags themselves are still ValueHelpers either way, so test .raw --
  "$alert.ends is not None" is always true and always was.
  $nwsforecast.alert_window() is unchanged and is still the right way to ask:
  it hands back (onset, finish, open_ended), with finish falling back to the
  expiry exactly as before, so a skin using it needs nothing.
- Fleet mode (read_from_dir) now sanity-checks the forecast files it reads,
  the same way a reply from NWS is checked.  A bad file is logged and retried
  instead of throwing a stack trace that named nothing, every
  retry_wait_secs, for as long as the file sat there.
- An alert's severity is now NAMED on its card, in a chip beside the event --
  SEVERE, MODERATE -- where 6.0 had a bare colored dot.  The severity had been
  reaching the reader as color and nothing else: a rail color and that dot,
  with the word appearing once, sixth in a six-item gray footer.  Color alone
  tells a colorblind reader nothing, and it mattered more here than that rule
  suggests -- the cards are SORTED by severity and the line above them says
  "most serious first", so the page announced that the order meant something
  while keeping the key in eight-point gray.  The rail now reinforces the
  chip rather than being the only place the severity lives, and the footer no
  longer repeats it; certainty and urgency stay there.
  One dark color moved to pay for it.  A severity is text now, so it needs a
  contrast bar it never had: --fc-sev-moderate went from #c6631c to #ca661f
  (4.32 to 4.51 against the card).  Light is unchanged, and the severity
  ordering is unchanged in both palettes.
- Fixed: ONE alert with no onset threw away EVERY alert for the station.  NWS
  need not say when an event begins, and the sanity check treated a missing
  onset as a malformed reply -- so the whole batch was discarded and the
  alerts page went on showing the previous one until NWS sent a batch without
  one.  In a national snapshot that was 3 alerts of 356, two of them
  Evacuation Immediate.  The failure was silent: a rejected reply logs and
  keeps the old data, so the page still looked current.
  The principle behind the fix, measured against that snapshot: NWS always
  says when it SPOKE -- sent, effective and expires were present on all 356 --
  and does not always say when the WEATHER starts or stops (onset absent on 3,
  ends on 23).  Message times are required; event times may be absent and are
  now stored as the nothing NWS sent, rather than as a message time wearing an
  event's name.  That also retires a guess: alert_window() used to infer an
  open-ended alert from its end EQUALLING its expiry, which was wrong for an
  alert that genuinely ends when its message does.
  An absent event time falls back to the message time that answers the same
  question, and that happens in alert_window() rather than in the database:
  no onset means in effect from `effective`, which is CAP's own reading, and
  no end means bounded by `expires`, which is what open_ended reports.  The
  onset half matters more than it sounds -- without it is_active() is false
  for every alert that has no onset, so alert_state() would file an
  Evacuation Immediate that is in effect RIGHT NOW under "upcoming", and
  two of the three null-onset alerts in the sample were exactly that event.
  ordered() ranks such an alert at the same instant, so the order a reader
  sees agrees with the window each card draws.
- The nws database now rebuilds itself when its schema is out of date.  6.1
  changes the schema (startTime and endTime become nullable, for the above),
  and earlier releases changed it too -- and the instruction was always "stop
  weewx, delete nws.sdb, start it again", with the extension doing nothing at
  all until you did.  It now notices at startup, says what differs, drops the
  table and lets WeeWX recreate it; the next poll refills it.  Nothing is lost
  that was ever read: no page looks at a row older than the current forecast.
  The check that finds it compares column NULLABILITY as well as names now.
  Names alone could not see this release's change at all, and would have
  reported an old table as current -- after which the first alert with no
  onset would have failed its INSERT, once per archive period, forever.
- Fixed: a report set to metric got Fahrenheit and miles per hour.  Every
  value the $nwsforecast tags returned came back in the units NWS served --
  degF and mph -- whatever [Units] said, so a metric page printed a
  Fahrenheit number beside a degC label, and an mph number beside a km/h one.
  The cause was that the tags built their ValueHelpers without the report's
  converter, and a ValueHelper converts once, when it is constructed, and
  never again: a helper built without one holds degF for life, which is why
  neither .raw nor .format() could save a skin from it.  They are now built
  with the report's own converter and formatter, like every other WeeWX tag,
  so a bare $hour.outTemp prints "71.0degF" through your skin's own
  [Units][[StringFormats]] and [[Labels]] rather than as a six-decimal number.
- A forecast reply that does not say it is in US units is now REJECTED rather
  than stored.  nws.py sends no units parameter and api.weather.gov answers
  "us", but a forecast url pasted into weewx.conf can carry "?units=si" -- and
  such a reply used to be composed into records stamped METRIC that nothing
  downstream ever read, so Celsius would have been served as Fahrenheit.  The
  log line names the cause and says what to change.
- The sample report's seven-day page now leads with TWO weeks of temperature:
  the week this station recorded, then the week the National Weather Service
  forecasts, on one shared scale.  A single scale is the point -- a cold snap
  last week rescales the forecast half, and "warm for the week" and "warm for
  the fortnight" are different claims that only one scale can tell apart.  The
  join is marked and named, the two curves are deliberately NOT connected
  across it (NWS forecasts a grid square and your station measures its own
  back yard, so the last reading and the first forecast hour seldom agree, and
  that step is worth seeing), and the observed half is drawn in a neutral ink
  rather than the forecast's red.  Pointing at the chart says which of the two
  a reading is.
  A station that has not been running for a week gets exactly the history it
  HAS -- three days of archive draw three days of chart, not a week four
  sevenths of which is invented -- and a station with no archive at all, which
  is every fresh install, gets precisely the forecast-only chart of 6.0.  An
  hour the station recorded nothing in leaves a hole in the line rather than a
  confident stroke across it, so an outage reads as an outage.
  The chart takes its own card, headed "Temperature, recorded and forecast",
  above the seven forecast day rows; the archive is read once per report
  through the report's own data binding, and an archive that cannot be read
  costs the chart and never the page.
  Note for upgrades: reinstalling overwrites skins/nws/ as it always has, so
  copy anything you customized before upgrading.  No configuration changes,
  and no change to any $nwsforecast tag.

6.0 09/01/2026
- The sample report is rebuilt.  Its three pages -- seven day, hourly and
  alerts -- are responsive, so one page serves a phone and a desktop, and they
  follow the reader's own light or dark setting.  The seven-day page leads with
  a "Right now" card and a sparkline of the week, then one row per CALENDAR DAY
  carrying that day's low-to-high bar, its daylight, and each period's icon,
  chance of rain, forecast and wind.  The hourly page gives the next twelve
  hours at a glance, then a tab per day carrying that day's chart --
  temperature, dew point and rain -- and its hour table, with the whole week as
  one curve at the foot.  The alerts page gives each alert a card with a
  severity-colored rail, a live status badge, a bar showing where now falls in
  the alert's window, and the description's own sections as real structure.
  The pages fetch nothing: no request to api.weather.gov, no font or script
  from anywhere else, and no reloading of themselves.
  Note for upgrades: reinstalling overwrites skins/nws/ as it always has, so
  copy anything you customized before upgrading.  The skin gained css/nws.css
  and scripts/nws.js and lost style.inc; weectl adds and overwrites but never
  removes, so a stale skins/nws/style.inc is left behind.  Nothing reads it.
- The charts can be driven from the keyboard.  Each carries tabindex="0", so
  Tab reaches it; the arrow keys then walk the same crosshair a pointer
  drives, Shift with an arrow moves six points at a time, Home and End jump
  to the ends, and Escape clears the reading.  Arrow keys are only taken
  while the chart has focus, so the page scrolls normally everywhere else.
  This makes the charts OPERABLE without a mouse, which is a smaller claim
  than making them accessible to a screen reader: no ARIA semantics were
  changed, the day tabs were already keyboard-operable as ordinary buttons,
  and the hour table remains a css grid rather than a real table.
- Ten new $nwsforecast tags, all returning DATA rather than markup.  They carry
  the NWS and CAP semantics that every skin would otherwise re-derive, and get
  subtly wrong: days() and hour_days() group periods by the calendar day each
  one STARTS in (walking day/night pairs instead puts one date on two rows and
  labels the leading one "Tonight" at dawn); points() unwraps hourly records
  into the plain numbers a chart needs; week_range() gives one shared
  temperature scale; line_label() keeps NWS's period name only where it says
  something the row does not; ordered(), is_active() and alert_window() rank
  alerts and reckon an alert's window, including the one in ten that never
  ends; parse_description() turns a CAP description into labeled sections; and
  nice_caps() title-cases NWS's shouted headlines without flattening its
  acronyms.  Nothing existing changed -- the tag surface only gained.  See the
  Tags page.
- parse_description() understands three more of the shapes NWS actually
  sends, so alert text stops reaching the page as raw punctuation.
  Starred lines whose label is not shouted are BULLETS, not prose: the warning
  products list their facts as "* Until 730 PM EDT." and "* At 651 PM EDT, a
  severe thunderstorm was located over...", and those were rendering with the
  asterisk still in them.  38 of 388 active alerts in a national snapshot
  carried such lines, 156 lines in all, led by Severe Thunderstorm Warning and
  Flash Flood Warning -- the most common warnings in the country.  A starred
  line whose label IS shouted is still a header, so "* WHAT...", "* CHANGES:"
  and "* LOCATIONS AFFECTED" are unaffected; the case split is the feed's own.
  Bare HAZARD..., SOURCE... and IMPACT... labels, with no asterisk at all, now
  become sections too -- 34 alerts, 102 labels.  A bare label must carry
  content after its ellipsis: without that guard a headline wrapping onto a
  line reading "NEVADA..." becomes a heading in the middle of a sentence.
  The tropical statements' **banner** lines are unwrapped.  They can wrap, so
  the closing pair lands on the next line and only meets the opening one after
  reflow, which is why it is stripped from the finished paragraph.
  Swept over the whole national feed: 391 alerts, 697 labeled sections, 904
  bullets, and no asterisk reaching a page.
- The sample report's own charts, chips and alert cards move into a new
  bin/user/nwsskin.py, reached from templates as $nwsskin.  It is presentation,
  it is explicitly NOT an API, and nothing in it is promised to survive a
  release -- copy what you like from it, but write your skin against
  $nwsforecast.
- Light and dark are derived from one written objective rather than picked:
  each color reproduces its light counterpart's prominence against the ground
  it actually sits on, holding hue and chroma.  Every rule is asserted over
  BOTH palettes and light must pass unchanged, which is what shows the rules
  describe the design rather than having been fitted to the new numbers -- and
  is how a live contrast failure in LIGHT mode was found and fixed.  There is
  deliberately no theme toggle: a toggle needs javascript, storage and a
  visible control, and everyone copying the skin would inherit a widget they
  may not want.
- Fixed: the alerts page could head its cards with a count they disproved --
  "3 alerts in effect" above a card reading EXPIRED.  The count is now
  recomputed in the browser alongside the badges, and an alert that has ENDED
  is counted as neither in effect nor beginning later, which it never was.
- New tag: $nwsforecast.alert_state() returns 'active', 'upcoming' or 'ended'
  for one alert -- the whole of the question is_active() answers half of.  The
  three are total and mutually exclusive, and the middle rule is the one that
  matters: an alert has ENDED when its finish is known and now is past it,
  WHATEVER the onset says.  Writing that as "started but not active" is the
  obvious way and it is wrong, because NWS does not always give an onset and
  an alert without one whose window has closed never "started".  Two skins
  wrote this classification separately and both got it wrong, from opposite
  directions, which is the argument for it being a tag.
  Fixed with it: an alert with no onset whose window had closed was described
  three different ways by the same page -- counted as upcoming, badged "Not
  yet begun", and skipped entirely by the page script, which selected only
  cards carrying an onset attribute.  All three now share one classification,
  and the python and javascript were driven over every shape the feed can
  produce and compared character for character.
  Worth knowing where the ended state actually arises: a freshly generated
  page never holds an ended alert, because rows whose endTime has passed are
  dropped as the tags read them.  It arises from the CLOCK -- a tab left open
  crosses an alert's end -- which is why the page script recomputes rather
  than trusting what generation baked.
- weewx-nws now draws its own weather icons.  $nwsforecast.icon() turns a
  forecast period's icon URL into an <svg> naming one of 68 drawn symbols (34
  NWS conditions, day and night), $nwsforecast.icon_sprite emits the symbol
  definitions those refer to, and $nwsforecast.icon_name() hands back the
  (condition, is_night, known) parsed out of the URL for a skin that would
  rather draw its own.  Drawn icons stay crisp at any size, take their colors
  from your stylesheet, and avoid the split "X then Y" composites the
  photographs produce, which read as rendering glitches at the size a forecast
  table uses.
  Three sets of names are a contract and will not change without a major
  version: the symbol ids (wx-<condition>-<day|night>), the css classes on the
  emitted markup (wxi, wxi-fallback, wxi-unknown) and the --wx-* custom
  properties that color the drawings.  See the Tags and Recipes pages.
- The sample report draws its icons instead of hot-linking api.weather.gov.
  Its pages now make no request to NWS at all.  css/nws.css sizes them per
  context -- 96px on the "Right now" card, 40px on a day row, 34px in the hour
  table.
- Every color in the drawn set is emitted as var(--wx-name, #default), so a
  skin can recolor any part of it and one that defines nothing renders the
  shipped look.  A complete dark palette ships as nwsicons.DARK, with a
  dark_css() helper that emits it as a paste-able rule; the Recipes page lists
  it in full.  It is derived rather than hand-picked: it reproduces the light
  palette's relative prominence structure on a dark ground, holding hue and
  chroma so heat stays red and the sun stays gold.  The cloud ramp therefore
  inverts -- on dark the front cloud is the darkest, because it is the least
  prominent one in light -- which reads better at small sizes than what it
  replaced.  Two tests assert the rules over BOTH palettes, stated in terms of
  how far a color stands from its page, so severe weather can never end up
  quieter than an ordinary cloud again.
- Fixed: the "overcast" symbol was a flat silhouette on a light page.  Its two
  cloud layers differed by 1.4 luma, because the back layer was drawn at 75%
  opacity rather than as a color, and a translucent fill composites against
  the page rather than against a color.  Those tonal opacities are solid ramp
  steps now (measured 28 luma apart, on any background); genuine translucency
  is kept and tunable via --wx-op-band and --wx-op-tube.
- Fixed: a hurricane's or tropical storm's eye was painted white, so it showed
  as a white blob on any page that was not white.  The center is simply left
  unpainted now and the page shows through.
- A forecast period with a blank icon URL renders the empty icon box rather
  than a clear-sky icon.  Alert records carry no icon, so a skin calling
  $nwsforecast.icon($alert.iconUrl) would otherwise have shown a sun over a
  tornado warning.
- A condition with no drawn symbol falls back to NWS's own image and is logged
  once per weewxd run.  Coverage is complete as of this release -- the drawn
  set, the conditions NWS publishes and their descriptions all match exactly --
  so the only way this can fire is NWS adding a condition, which is worth
  hearing about.  tests/test_nwsicons.py checks the set against
  https://api.weather.gov/icons itself rather than against a copied list; run
  it with `pytest tests -m network` (the rest of the suite stays offline).
- An `unknown` icon no longer discards the whole forecast.  NWS sometimes gives
  a period the icon name `unknown`, meaning it has none for that period.  That
  used to fail the entire reply in the sanity check -- throwing away up to 156
  periods of good data over one missing glyph, and leaving the site serving the
  previous forecast until NWS sent a clean one.  Such periods are now stored
  and logged once per reply with a count.  They render as an empty icon box:
  api.weather.gov answers 400 for that URL, so it must not be linked.
- Remove the 204 bundled icon photographs.  They were installed and copied into
  the report's HTML_ROOT but no template ever referenced them.  Nothing that
  worked before stops working.
  Note for upgrades: weectl only adds and overwrites, so the 204 files a 5.1 or
  earlier install put in skins/nws/nws_icons/ and in the report's HTML_ROOT are
  left behind.  Nothing reads them.  They can be deleted at leisure:
    rm -r /home/weewx/skins/nws/nws_icons /home/weewx/public_html/nws/nws_icons
- weewx-nws now has a user manual, at
  https://chaunceygardiner.github.io/weewx-nws/ -- installation, configuration,
  every $nwsforecast tag and field, the sample report, gridpoints, the
  command-line utilities and troubleshooting, with search.  The README is now a
  summary that links to it, and the [NWS] section the installer writes points
  there as well.
- Fix an HTML validation warning on the sample report's alerts page: the spacer
  row at the top of an alert's details table was one column wide, so the Nu
  Html Checker warned on every two-column row (Issued, Onset, ...) that
  followed.  The warnings only appeared when an alert was active.
- Add tests/validate_skin_html.py: renders the sample skin (both with and
  without an active alert) and validates every page with the Nu Html Checker.
  It would have caught the warning above regardless of the day's weather; it
  requires java and vnu.jar, and is not part of the pytest suite.
- The installer now writes the [NWS] section of weewx.conf with comments
  explaining every option, and writes each option that merely selects a default
  (days_to_keep, poll_secs, alert_poll_secs, retry_wait_secs,
  alert_retry_wait_secs and timeout_secs) commented out, showing the default
  that governs.  Leave such a line commented and weewx-nws's own value governs,
  including a better one a later release might bring; uncomment it to pin the
  station to the value written there.  data_binding stays live, as data
  bindings are everywhere else in weewx.conf.  User-Agent stays live too and is
  now marked as the placeholder it has always been -- it is the one option that
  must be edited.  This is a FRESH INSTALL change only: upgrading never
  rewrites weewx.conf, so existing stations are untouched.  No default has
  changed.

5.1 07/12/2026
- Fix shutdown: weewx could fail to stop when the SIGTERM that stops weewxd
  landed while the main thread was inside weewx-nws's startup or
  end-of-archive-period database code — the broad exception handlers there
  logged the shutdown exception and continued.  Those handlers now pass the

1.7 01/28/2022
1. Revamped support for sharing forecasts between instances.

   a. Master weewx instances (i.e., instances of weewx that will
      query NWS for forecasts and distribute them), should add
      the following to the NWS section in weewx.conf:

   [NWS]
       .
       .
       .
       [[RsyncSpec]]
           enable = true
           remote_clients = <weewx-machine-1>, <weewx-machine-2>
           remote_user = root
           remote_dir = /root/forecasts
           compress = false
           log_success = false
           ssh_options = -o ConnectTimeout=1
           timeout = 1

   b. client weewx instances that will read forecast files distributed
      by the master (auto-magically via ssh), need to add the following
      to the NWS section of weewx.conf:

   [NWS]
       .
       .
       .
       read_from_dir = /root/forecasts

    c. passwordless ssh also needs to be setup between the master and the
       clients from the user on the master to the user specified by
       remote_user.  If you don't understand this, this feature is not for
       you.

1.6 01/23/2022
1. Support sharing forecasts between instances.  To accomplish this,
   a. for the "master" machine (i.e., the instance that will make
      forecasts available to share), add the following to the NWS
      section:
        forecasts_dir = <directory-to-write-TWELVE_HOUR-and-ONE_HOUR-forecasts>
   b. distribute the TWELVE_HOUR and ONE_HOUR files on your own (with rsync or scp commands in crontab)
   c. on the machines where the TWELVE_HOUR and ONE_HOUR files are distributed, add
      the following to the NWS section:
        read_twelve_hour_forecast_from_file = /<path>/TWELVE_HOUR
        read_one_hour_forecast_from_file = /<path>/ONE_HOUR
   With the above setup, only one instance (the master) will actually call nws.
   Note: It is not anticipated that this feature will be popular, as such, it is not described in the README.
