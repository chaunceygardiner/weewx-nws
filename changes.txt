nws change history
------------------

6.0 (in progress)
- The sample report is rebuilt.  Its three pages -- seven day, hourly and
  alerts -- are responsive, so one page serves a phone and a desktop, and they
  follow the reader's own light or dark setting.  The seven-day page leads with
  a "Right now" card and a sparkline of the week, then one row per CALENDAR DAY
  carrying that day's low-to-high bar, its daylight, and each period's icon,
  chance of rain, forecast and wind.  The hourly page gives the next twelve
  hours at a glance, then a tab per day carrying that day's chart --
  temperature, dew point and rain -- and its hour table, with the whole week as
  one curve at the foot.  The alerts page gives each alert a card with a
  severity-colored rail, a live status badge, a bar showing where now falls in
  the alert's window, and the description's own sections as real structure.
  The pages fetch nothing: no request to api.weather.gov, no font or script
  from anywhere else, and no reloading of themselves.
  Note for upgrades: reinstalling overwrites skins/nws/ as it always has, so
  copy anything you customized before upgrading.  The skin gained css/nws.css
  and scripts/nws.js and lost style.inc; weectl adds and overwrites but never
  removes, so a stale skins/nws/style.inc is left behind.  Nothing reads it.
- The charts can be driven from the keyboard.  Each carries tabindex="0", so
  Tab reaches it; the arrow keys then walk the same crosshair a pointer
  drives, Shift with an arrow moves six points at a time, Home and End jump
  to the ends, and Escape clears the reading.  Arrow keys are only taken
  while the chart has focus, so the page scrolls normally everywhere else.
  This makes the charts OPERABLE without a mouse, which is a smaller claim
  than making them accessible to a screen reader: no ARIA semantics were
  changed, the day tabs were already keyboard-operable as ordinary buttons,
  and the hour table remains a css grid rather than a real table.
- Ten new $nwsforecast tags, all returning DATA rather than markup.  They carry
  the NWS and CAP semantics that every skin would otherwise re-derive, and get
  subtly wrong: days() and hour_days() group periods by the calendar day each
  one STARTS in (walking day/night pairs instead puts one date on two rows and
  labels the leading one "Tonight" at dawn); points() unwraps hourly records
  into the plain numbers a chart needs; week_range() gives one shared
  temperature scale; line_label() keeps NWS's period name only where it says
  something the row does not; ordered(), is_active() and alert_window() rank
  alerts and reckon an alert's window, including the one in ten that never
  ends; parse_description() turns a CAP description into labeled sections; and
  nice_caps() title-cases NWS's shouted headlines without flattening its
  acronyms.  Nothing existing changed -- the tag surface only gained.  See the
  Tags page.
- parse_description() understands three more of the shapes NWS actually
  sends, so alert text stops reaching the page as raw punctuation.
  Starred lines whose label is not shouted are BULLETS, not prose: the warning
  products list their facts as "* Until 730 PM EDT." and "* At 651 PM EDT, a
  severe thunderstorm was located over...", and those were rendering with the
  asterisk still in them.  38 of 388 active alerts in a national snapshot
  carried such lines, 156 lines in all, led by Severe Thunderstorm Warning and
  Flash Flood Warning -- the most common warnings in the country.  A starred
  line whose label IS shouted is still a header, so "* WHAT...", "* CHANGES:"
  and "* LOCATIONS AFFECTED" are unaffected; the case split is the feed's own.
  Bare HAZARD..., SOURCE... and IMPACT... labels, with no asterisk at all, now
  become sections too -- 34 alerts, 102 labels.  A bare label must carry
  content after its ellipsis: without that guard a headline wrapping onto a
  line reading "NEVADA..." becomes a heading in the middle of a sentence.
  The tropical statements' **banner** lines are unwrapped.  They can wrap, so
  the closing pair lands on the next line and only meets the opening one after
  reflow, which is why it is stripped from the finished paragraph.
  Swept over the whole national feed: 391 alerts, 697 labeled sections, 904
  bullets, and no asterisk reaching a page.
- The sample report's own charts, chips and alert cards move into a new
  bin/user/nwsskin.py, reached from templates as $nwsskin.  It is presentation,
  it is explicitly NOT an API, and nothing in it is promised to survive a
  release -- copy what you like from it, but write your skin against
  $nwsforecast.
- Light and dark are derived from one written objective rather than picked:
  each color reproduces its light counterpart's prominence against the ground
  it actually sits on, holding hue and chroma.  Every rule is asserted over
  BOTH palettes and light must pass unchanged, which is what shows the rules
  describe the design rather than having been fitted to the new numbers -- and
  is how a live contrast failure in LIGHT mode was found and fixed.  There is
  deliberately no theme toggle: a toggle needs javascript, storage and a
  visible control, and everyone copying the skin would inherit a widget they
  may not want.
- Fixed: the alerts page could head its cards with a count they disproved --
  "3 alerts in effect" above a card reading EXPIRED.  The count is now
  recomputed in the browser alongside the badges, and an alert that has ENDED
  is counted as neither in effect nor beginning later, which it never was.
- New tag: $nwsforecast.alert_state() returns 'active', 'upcoming' or 'ended'
  for one alert -- the whole of the question is_active() answers half of.  The
  three are total and mutually exclusive, and the middle rule is the one that
  matters: an alert has ENDED when its finish is known and now is past it,
  WHATEVER the onset says.  Writing that as "started but not active" is the
  obvious way and it is wrong, because NWS does not always give an onset and
  an alert without one whose window has closed never "started".  Two skins
  wrote this classification separately and both got it wrong, from opposite
  directions, which is the argument for it being a tag.
  Fixed with it: an alert with no onset whose window had closed was described
  three different ways by the same page -- counted as upcoming, badged "Not
  yet begun", and skipped entirely by the page script, which selected only
  cards carrying an onset attribute.  All three now share one classification,
  and the python and javascript were driven over every shape the feed can
  produce and compared character for character.
  Worth knowing where the ended state actually arises: a freshly generated
  page never holds an ended alert, because rows whose endTime has passed are
  dropped as the tags read them.  It arises from the CLOCK -- a tab left open
  crosses an alert's end -- which is why the page script recomputes rather
  than trusting what generation baked.
- weewx-nws now draws its own weather icons.  $nwsforecast.icon() turns a
  forecast period's icon URL into an <svg> naming one of 68 drawn symbols (34
  NWS conditions, day and night), $nwsforecast.icon_sprite emits the symbol
  definitions those refer to, and $nwsforecast.icon_name() hands back the
  (condition, is_night, known) parsed out of the URL for a skin that would
  rather draw its own.  Drawn icons stay crisp at any size, take their colors
  from your stylesheet, and avoid the split "X then Y" composites the
  photographs produce, which read as rendering glitches at the size a forecast
  table uses.
  Three sets of names are a contract and will not change without a major
  version: the symbol ids (wx-<condition>-<day|night>), the css classes on the
  emitted markup (wxi, wxi-fallback, wxi-unknown) and the --wx-* custom
  properties that color the drawings.  See the Tags and Recipes pages.
- The sample report draws its icons instead of hot-linking api.weather.gov.
  Its pages now make no request to NWS at all.  css/nws.css sizes them per
  context -- 96px on the "Right now" card, 40px on a day row, 34px in the hour
  table.
- Every color in the drawn set is emitted as var(--wx-name, #default), so a
  skin can recolor any part of it and one that defines nothing renders the
  shipped look.  A complete dark palette ships as nwsicons.DARK, with a
  dark_css() helper that emits it as a paste-able rule; the Recipes page lists
  it in full.  It is derived rather than hand-picked: it reproduces the light
  palette's relative prominence structure on a dark ground, holding hue and
  chroma so heat stays red and the sun stays gold.  The cloud ramp therefore
  inverts -- on dark the front cloud is the darkest, because it is the least
  prominent one in light -- which reads better at small sizes than what it
  replaced.  Two tests assert the rules over BOTH palettes, stated in terms of
  how far a color stands from its page, so severe weather can never end up
  quieter than an ordinary cloud again.
- Fixed: the "overcast" symbol was a flat silhouette on a light page.  Its two
  cloud layers differed by 1.4 luma, because the back layer was drawn at 75%
  opacity rather than as a color, and a translucent fill composites against
  the page rather than against a color.  Those tonal opacities are solid ramp
  steps now (measured 28 luma apart, on any background); genuine translucency
  is kept and tunable via --wx-op-band and --wx-op-tube.
- Fixed: a hurricane's or tropical storm's eye was painted white, so it showed
  as a white blob on any page that was not white.  The center is simply left
  unpainted now and the page shows through.
- A forecast period with a blank icon URL renders the empty icon box rather
  than a clear-sky icon.  Alert records carry no icon, so a skin calling
  $nwsforecast.icon($alert.iconUrl) would otherwise have shown a sun over a
  tornado warning.
- A condition with no drawn symbol falls back to NWS's own image and is logged
  once per weewxd run.  Coverage is complete as of this release -- the drawn
  set, the conditions NWS publishes and their descriptions all match exactly --
  so the only way this can fire is NWS adding a condition, which is worth
  hearing about.  tests/test_nwsicons.py checks the set against
  https://api.weather.gov/icons itself rather than against a copied list; run
  it with `pytest tests -m network` (the rest of the suite stays offline).
- An `unknown` icon no longer discards the whole forecast.  NWS sometimes gives
  a period the icon name `unknown`, meaning it has none for that period.  That
  used to fail the entire reply in the sanity check -- throwing away up to 156
  periods of good data over one missing glyph, and leaving the site serving the
  previous forecast until NWS sent a clean one.  Such periods are now stored
  and logged once per reply with a count.  They render as an empty icon box:
  api.weather.gov answers 400 for that URL, so it must not be linked.
- Remove the 204 bundled icon photographs.  They were installed and copied into
  the report's HTML_ROOT but no template ever referenced them.  Nothing that
  worked before stops working.
  Note for upgrades: weectl only adds and overwrites, so the 204 files a 5.1 or
  earlier install put in skins/nws/nws_icons/ and in the report's HTML_ROOT are
  left behind.  Nothing reads them.  They can be deleted at leisure:
    rm -r /home/weewx/skins/nws/nws_icons /home/weewx/public_html/nws/nws_icons
- weewx-nws now has a user manual, at
  https://chaunceygardiner.github.io/weewx-nws/ -- installation, configuration,
  every $nwsforecast tag and field, the sample report, gridpoints, the
  command-line utilities and troubleshooting, with search.  The README is now a
  summary that links to it, and the [NWS] section the installer writes points
  there as well.
- Fix an HTML validation warning on the sample report's alerts page: the spacer
  row at the top of an alert's details table was one column wide, so the Nu
  Html Checker warned on every two-column row (Issued, Onset, ...) that
  followed.  The warnings only appeared when an alert was active.
- Add tests/validate_skin_html.py: renders the sample skin (both with and
  without an active alert) and validates every page with the Nu Html Checker.
  It would have caught the warning above regardless of the day's weather; it
  requires java and vnu.jar, and is not part of the pytest suite.
- The installer now writes the [NWS] section of weewx.conf with comments
  explaining every option, and writes each option that merely selects a default
  (days_to_keep, poll_secs, alert_poll_secs, retry_wait_secs,
  alert_retry_wait_secs and timeout_secs) commented out, showing the default
  that governs.  Leave such a line commented and weewx-nws's own value governs,
  including a better one a later release might bring; uncomment it to pin the
  station to the value written there.  data_binding stays live, as data
  bindings are everywhere else in weewx.conf.  User-Agent stays live too and is
  now marked as the placeholder it has always been -- it is the one option that
  must be edited.  This is a FRESH INSTALL change only: upgrading never
  rewrites weewx.conf, so existing stations are untouched.  No default has
  changed.

5.1 07/12/2026
- Fix shutdown: weewx could fail to stop when the SIGTERM that stops weewxd
  landed while the main thread was inside weewx-nws's startup or
  end-of-archive-period database code — the broad exception handlers there
  logged the shutdown exception and continued.  Those handlers now pass the
  shutdown through.

5.0 07/12/2026
- weewx-nws now requires WeeWX 5 and Python 3.9 (WeeWX 4 support removed).
- Fix windDir for ENE forecasts: it was reported as 77.5 degrees; ENE is 67.5 degrees.
- Fix the hourly page of the sample report: a ranged wind speed (e.g. "2 to 9 mph")
  rendered literal template text due to a missing $ in hours.html.tmpl.
- --test-service now honors the --binding option (it was accepted but ignored since it
  was introduced in 2020; test-service's inline config also used the key 'binding'
  where the service reads 'data_binding').
- Sanity check forecasts for a well formed geometry (used for the gridpoint polygon
  check).  A forecast without one was already rejected, but at compose time and
  without logging the raw response.
- Fold the check_grid.py utility into nws.py.  It no longer requires matplotlib.
  Run with: python bin/user/nws.py --check-grid --latitude <lat> --longitude <long>
  The standalone check_grid.py has been removed.
- Add a test suite (tests/).  Run from the repo root, with a python in which
  WeeWX is installed: python -m pytest tests
  Also added tests/verify_cli.py, a harness that runs every nws.py command-line
  option against live NWS and reports PASS/FAIL per option.

        twelve_hour_forecast_url = "https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/MTR/91,87/forecast"

1.10 04/25/2022
1. Emit error in log if returned NWS forecast is the wrong grid (due to a long standing NWS bug.
   If you see a message in the log such as:
   "WARNING user.nws: Lat/Long 37.431495/-122.110937 does not fall within bounds of forecast's polygon (due to NWS Bug)."
   your grid is off by (1,1).  In this example, you'll see in the log that the grid being called is 92,88.
       INFO user.nws: Downloading ForecastType.TWELVE_HOUR forecasts from https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/MTR/91,87/forecast.
       INFO user.nws: Downloading ForecastType.ONE_HOUR forecasts from https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/MTR/92,88/forecast/hourly.
   It should be 91,87.  As such, add the following lines to the NWS section of weewx.conf:
       twelve_hour_forecast_url = "https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/MTR/91,87/forecast"
       one_hour_forecast_url = "https://api.weather.gov/gridpoints/MTR/91,87/forecast/hourly"

1.7 01/28/2022
1. Revamped support for sharing forecasts between instances.

   a. Master weewx instances (i.e., instances of weewx that will
      query NWS for forecasts and distribute them), should add
      the following to the NWS section in weewx.conf:

   [NWS]
       .
       .
       .
       [[RsyncSpec]]
           enable = true
           remote_clients = <weewx-machine-1>, <weewx-machine-2>
           remote_user = root
           remote_dir = /root/forecasts
           compress = false
           log_success = false
           ssh_options = -o ConnectTimeout=1
           timeout = 1

   b. client weewx instances that will read forecast files distributed
      by the master (auto-magically via ssh), need to add the following
      to the NWS section of weewx.conf:

   [NWS]
       .
       .
       .
       read_from_dir = /root/forecasts

    c. passwordless ssh also needs to be setup between the master and the
       clients from the user on the master to the user specified by
       remote_user.  If you don't understand this, this feature is not for
       you.

1.6 01/23/2022
1. Support sharing forecasts between instances.  To accomplish this,
   a. for the "master" machine (i.e., the instance that will make
      forecasts available to share), add the following to the NWS
      section:
        forecasts_dir = <directory-to-write-TWELVE_HOUR-and-ONE_HOUR-forecasts>
   b. distribute the TWELVE_HOUR and ONE_HOUR files on your own (with rsync or scp commands in crontab)
   c. on the machines where the TWELVE_HOUR and ONE_HOUR files are distributed, add
      the following to the NWS section:
        read_twelve_hour_forecast_from_file = /<path>/TWELVE_HOUR
        read_one_hour_forecast_from_file = /<path>/ONE_HOUR
   With the above setup, only one instance (the master) will actually call nws.
   Note: It is not anticipated that this feature will be popular, as such, it is not described in the README.
